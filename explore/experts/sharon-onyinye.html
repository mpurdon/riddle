Product designer · design educator · founder of Coachli

Sharon Onyinye

A self-taught designer from Lagos who became a senior product designer in Berlin and London, taught a million viewers how to start, and then built a payments platform for African creators in a low-trust market. Her lesson for Riddle: learn the vocabulary first, define success before you design, and earn trust by showing people exactly what happened to the thing they care about.

Major ideas

Ten ideas, each traceable to her own material

Her teaching is practical rather than theoretical: how to start, how to argue for a design, how to work with a product manager, and, since founding Coachli, how trust is earned when people cannot see their money. Where a point rests on a video's chapter list rather than its words, the card says so.

2018 · 2021

Learn the discipline before the tool

Beginners should not start by opening Figma. Her 2018 "Getting Started With UI Design" and 2021 "What is UX Design?" both begin by naming the parts (usability, visual design, information architecture, interaction design, research) and a six-step process before any software. She made a separate explainer on UX terms every designer should know: vocabulary is a deliberate first lesson.

Example. Her most-watched video lists free UX fundamentals, then visual design, then Daily UI, then fake-client challenges, in that order.
Riddle asks people to learn five new nouns before they see an email. Teach them once, in plain words, at first run.
2021 · 2024

You are not your user; define success first

Knowing the user is step two of her process, before ideation. Her HelloFresh challenge is chaptered "Problem Discovery: Data and Insights", "Measuring success", "How might we", then wireframes, then "Conclusion: Measuring Success" again. Success metrics are set before design and revisited at the end. A 2024 video on data and metrics for designers continues the theme.

Example. Her 2025 career retro credits her growth to customer research, data-driven decisions and running discovery workshops.
Pick the triage metrics before polishing screens: time to an empty Finds list, Unresolved share, undo rate.
2020 · 2024

A case study is a process story

Her most developed practical material: a portfolio must contain three things, a case study eight; five dos and four don'ts. The presentation that won her a senior role is chaptered as introduction, experience, skills, design process, projects. Interviewers hire judgement, and judgement is only visible when you show the problem, the constraints, the options you rejected and how you would know it worked.

Example. "My UX Design Case Study Presentation that Got Me Hired", 2024, 57,000 views. The exact eight items are known from chapter markers only.
A Director proposal should read like a case study: problem, evidence, the diff, alternatives, how success will be judged.
2019

Reuse patterns; people remember repeated flows

UI patterns are reusable solutions to recurring problems: input and output, navigation, content structure, sharing, onboarding. Users benefit because a familiar pattern needs no learning; designers benefit because it is a solved problem. Her worked example is autosave, which removes a whole class of error-recovery UI by preventing the loss in the first place.

Example. "Speed up Your Workflow with UI Patterns", Medium. Consistency and recognisability first; novelty only where it earns its place.
Single-click opens a Find but double-click opens a message. That is a pattern break people will remember.
2025

Semantic tokens, with AI as the starting point

A concrete workflow: ask an assistant for a starter palette from the brand and accessibility needs, have it structure primitives and semantic tokens as JSON where semantic tokens reference primitives rather than hex values, import into Figma variables, then wire the semantic layer into component fills and states, because tokens in a panel do nothing on their own. AI removes the blank-page cost; the designer keeps the structural decisions.

Example. "Using AI To Define My Colour Tokens & Figma Variables In 30 Minutes", November 2025. The post does not discuss AI failure modes.
Riddle's tokens are primitives (ochre, moss, rust). A semantic layer (disposition.find, state.unresolved) would let density and contrast variants change in one place.
2025

Evolve, pivot or die: taste and edge cases

Expectations of designers arrived in waves: visual craft, process rigour, business impact, taste, now AI-era speed. Output is cheap, so value lies in product thinking, shipping, systems thinking, decision-making and taste as curation over generation, and in covering edge cases with tight flows. The review question is no longer "does it look right" but "is this the right problem and do the edge cases hold".

Example. "Dear Designer: Apparently You Must Evolve, Pivot or Die", December 2025.
Enumerate the edge cases before new features: a Dig with zero messages, a Mesh that never fires, a label removed in Gmail after Riddle applied it.
2025

Ask what it should make someone feel

A deliberately contrarian case: brand designers start from the feeling a thing should evoke, product designers from constraints and components, which has made product design safe and forgettable. Her remedy is doodling, mood boards and fake briefs to recover holistic thinking. Read with the patterns idea, it is a sequence: patterns for the mechanics, feeling for the identity.

Example. "Going Back to Brand Design to Become a Better Designer", November 2025.
Decide what a triage session should feel like (calm, methodical, in control) and test the archaeology metaphor against that.
2021 · 2024

Role clarity with product managers

Her soft-skills content is unusually specific: curiosity, empathy, communication and collaboration next to the hard skills; eight tips for working with product managers; a published "Roles and expectations workshop" Figma file linked from three videos. Most design failure is a disagreement about who decides what, so she makes roles explicit before the work.

Example. The Hanselminutes episode is framed around how designers interface with front-end developers.
Every disposition should show who decided: a Mesh, a Signal, the Never Hidden gate, the Director, or you.
2021 · 2025

Discipline, visibility and ownership

Passion only takes you so far; discipline is the consistent game-changer. Her 2025 lessons: ownership beats everything, invisible work does not exist, network inside the company first, feedback always stings, there is no dream job. Keep a brag doc because nobody else will remember your achievements as clearly as you.

Example. "8 Things I've Learned in 8 Years", October 2025. Careers are products: they need release notes, metrics and owners.
Activity is Riddle's brag doc. Let it tell the day's story in a sentence, so the value delivered is visible to the user.
2023 – 2026

Ship the simple version; trust is earned by visibility

Coachli's MVP was "incredibly simple" and users revealed they needed far more than planned. Her hard lesson: Nigeria is a low-trust environment around money, so people want to know where their money is and when they will get it. "Ideas Are Easy, Execution Is Everything" generalises it: one impactful task a day, track completed projects not plans, gradual implementation over perfect planning.

Example. Coachli grew from a booking tool into infrastructure for a category that had none, "designed for African economics".
Riddle's "money" is mail. Show "would have archived 14, labelled 9" before any write, and make Undo the most prominent control at Act.

Applied to Riddle

Twelve implications for Riddle's flows

  1. 1
    VocabularyTeach the nouns on first run, once, in plain words. A one-screen glossary in the add-account wizard and a persistent gloss in the rail ("Finds: needs you", "Unresolved: Riddle could not decide") so the metaphor is learned, not guessed. Keep the metaphor; do not make it the only label.From: Learn the discipline before the tool; Reuse patterns.
  2. 2
    OnboardingMake the wizard a recognisable onboarding pattern. Step count, one decision per step, and a closing "what happens next" screen that states the starting rung (Observe) and that nothing is written to Gmail yet.From: Reuse patterns.
  3. 3
    AutonomyTreat the autonomy ladder as a trust ledger. In Observe and Suggest, show "would have archived 14, labelled 9" in Activity before any write. Make Undo the most prominent control on Act and Trusted screens. Offer promotion only after a run of decisions the user did not undo.From: Ship the simple version; trust is earned by visibility.
  4. 4
    MetricsDefine triage success metrics before polishing screens. Time from sync to an empty Finds list, share of messages landing in Unresolved, undo rate per disposition, proposals accepted versus rejected. Surface them so the user and the team see the same numbers.From: You are not your user; define success first.
  5. 5
    ProcessRun the study she would run. Five to eight first-time users each connect one account and narrate what they think Finds, Unresolved and Spoil mean before opening them; then a timed task: "make a Mesh that files newsletters". Capture the result as a case study in the repo docs.From: You are not your user; Learn the discipline before the tool.
  6. 6
    CritiqueA per-screen checklist from her components. Usability (keyboard completes the core action?), information architecture (rail order matches frequency of use?), interaction (feedback immediate after every change?), hierarchy (next action obvious in a dense list?), edge cases, flow tightness (keystrokes from a Find to Filed).From: Learn the discipline before the tool; Evolve, pivot or die.
  7. 7
    VisualModel disposition colours as semantic tokens. Primitives, then disposition.find, disposition.spoil, state.unresolved, with components wired only to the semantic layer, so dark mode and density variants change in one place.From: Semantic tokens, with AI as the starting point.
  8. 8
    VocabularyDecide what a triage session should feel like. Pick the feeling and let it drive the Finds view's pacing, motion and empty-state copy. Where the metaphor fights clarity (Contamination versus Spoil is not obvious), let clarity win.From: Ask what it should make someone feel.
  9. 9
    DirectorPresent proposals in case-study shape. Problem statement, evidence (ids and counts, never bodies), the proposed diff, alternatives considered, and how success will be judged. Approval becomes a measurable decision rather than a yes on a diff.From: A case study is a process story.
  10. 10
    WhyShow who decided, inline. Activity already holds provenance. Expose it in list rows: which Mesh, which Signal, the Never Hidden gate, the Director, or you.From: Role clarity with product managers.
  11. 11
    PrioritiesCover the named edge cases before adding features. A Dig with zero messages; a Mesh whose condition never fires; a label removed in Gmail after Riddle applied it; undo after a later action touched the same thread; two Digs with conflicting Meshes.From: Evolve, pivot or die.
  12. 12
    DirectorShip the smallest Director and watch it. One proposal type (adjust a single Mesh), a visible acceptance count, then expand based on which proposals people accept. Keep the Activity view readable as a record of value delivered.From: Ship the simple version; Discipline, visibility and ownership.
