Three directions

The same Riddle, three ways of holding it

Each direction is a coherent answer to "what is Riddle for, at the moment you open it". They are not exclusive: the recommendations below mostly take the Deck's lists, the Brief's home and rhythm, and the Ledger's visibility. Pick the one that should win when they conflict.

DIRECTION A

The Brief

Riddle is a place you visit two or three times a day to review what it decided, not an inbox you live in. It opens on a brief: what needs you, what it filed, what it would have done at the next rung, what it noticed. A review walks you through Finds and Unresolved one at a time and ends with a summary.

  • Home: a daily brief with a "Start review" button
  • Rail: five destinations (Today, Needs you, Mail, Rules, Director)
  • Director: starts with an answer; proposals inline; chat collapsed
  • Rhythm: alerts only for allow-listed senders; everything else batched
Influences: HEY, Cora's Brief, Apple digests, the batching studies, Wroblewski's answer-first, Spool's seducible moment. Risk: people who want an inbox will feel steered.
DIRECTION B

The Deck

Riddle is a keyboard instrument. Every list behaves the same way, every row leads with its reason, single keys carry verdicts, a palette prints the keys, and the preview pane means you never leave the list. Speed is the trust feature.

  • Home: Finds, reason-first, with a preview pane
  • Rail: current destinations, regrouped, counts follow the account
  • Director: a proposal queue with 1/2/3 verdicts
  • Rhythm: unchanged; the loop is fast enough to visit often
Influences: Superhuman, Linear, GitHub's inbox, Raycast, Showalter's density rules. Risk: the learning curve; keys must mean one thing per object.
DIRECTION C

The Ledger

Riddle is an audited assistant. Every row shows who decided and what reached Gmail; the account's rung is always in the chrome; corrections become visible rules; proposals are case studies with counts. Activity is the home for anyone who does not yet trust the machine.

  • Home: Summary with a trust card and per-rule precision
  • Rail: unchanged, plus a rung chip and effect toasts everywhere
  • Director: proposals as problem, evidence, options, diff, expected effect
  • Rhythm: unchanged; the ledger is the source of calm
Influences: GitHub reason labels, Missive's rule timeline, Shneiderman's audit trail, Kulesza's explanatory debugging, Onyinye's trust ledger. Risk: more to read per row; can feel bureaucratic once trust is earned.

Twelve flows

Flow by flow: today, the options, the evidence, a recommendation

Severity is Nielsen's scale from the audit. "Effort" is a rough size for the recommended option: S is a day or two of frontend work, M is a week with one new Action, L is a multi-week change touching the engine. Wireframes are sketches of the recommended option; ochre marks what changes.

Flow 1 · Onboarding

First run and adding an account

Today. With no account, the Summary says "Nothing needs your attention." and offers Review Spoil and Undo 4. The tour walks three empty pages and promises a digest that no longer exists 3. The hardest step, Google Cloud, is a repository path in monospace 3. An expired sign-in has no way back except Remove, which wipes learned state 4. The wizard itself is good: one decision per step and an effect sentence.
OPTION A · BRIEF

Show first, ask later

First launch creates the sample mailbox in Observe, runs the default Meshes, and opens a populated Summary and Finds under a persistent banner: "This is sample mail. Connect Gmail when you are ready." The tour is replaced by three pull tips that appear on the first Find, the first Unresolved item and the first proposal, each dismissable and recallable from Help. The Google Cloud step opens the guide in the browser and lists the six console steps inline with a "Done" tick per step. The account panel gains "Sign in again".

Trade-off: a sample mailbox must be obviously a sample, or it will be mistaken for the user's own.
OPTION B · DECK

Guided setup

A full-screen first-run sequence: what Riddle does in three lines; connect Gmail with the console steps inline; choose the starting rung (Observe pre-selected); a sync screen that shows the work (messages fetched, rules evaluated, Finds so far) and lands in Finds. No sample data. Reconnect through the error card.

Trade-off: nothing to see until Google Cloud is done, which can take twenty minutes.
OPTION C · LEDGER

Empty state as instruction

Keep the structure. Gate the Summary and the Task Centre on zero accounts with one "Add an account" card, drop the digest sentence from the tour, add "Sign in again". Smallest change; fixes both severity-4 items.

Trade-off: still asks for Google Cloud before showing any value.
What the evidence says. Spool's $300 million button: every gate before value is a tax you only see by watching people hit it. Wroblewski: registration "postponed, or handled behind the scenes"; "let the AI do the onboarding" and start with a result. NN/g (2023): tours are skipped and forgotten; contextual pull help works. Buell and Norton: people value visible work during a wait, so a first sync should show what Riddle is doing. Superhuman's concierge configures the real inbox; HEY's forced re-screening of every sender is the counter-example. Kole Jain: onboarding is sequencing, "a single tooltip pointing at the most important action", then a second, then a checklist, never "the entire product in a modal with six bullet points"; close a shortcut tip by executing the shortcut. The audit's two severity-4 items both live here.
Recommendation: A, with C's gate as the floor. The sample mailbox already exists (the fixture provider); the change is to create it on first launch and label it loudly. "Sign in again" needs one Action that reuses the OAuth flow for an existing Dig and keeps its classifications; the auth error card should land on it.

Flow 2 · Navigation and vocabulary

The rail, the words, and what an account looks like in the chrome

Today. Four rail groups; "Excavation" is an unexplained heading over two review trays, a taxonomy, two policy editors and the audit log. Signals sits in primary navigation although its page speaks of Platt scaling. Spoil has a trash icon, Mesh a network icon 2. About 45 domain nouns, 16 before the daily loop makes sense. "Dig" in the app, "account" in Settings. Rail counts and the Summary ignore the account switcher 2. The account's rung is shown nowhere but Settings 3.
OPTION A · ANY DIRECTION

Regroup, gloss, and put the rung in the chrome

Rail groups become: Summary, Finds, Unresolved; Mail (Inbox, Spoil, Contamination); Rules (Meshes, Strata, Never Hidden); Director (Director, Expeditions); Activity, Settings. Signals moves under Meshes as a tab, visible in full only in developer mode. Icons: a sieve for Meshes, an archive box for Spoil. Every rail item and every chip carries a one-line gloss on hover until it has been used five times: "Spoil · low-value mail, archived in Gmail at Act". One word per concept in copy: account in Settings, Dig elsewhere, with the wizard sentence "each account is its own Dig" kept. The account switcher shows the rung and sync state, and rail counts follow it.

Trade-off: glosses add hover text the keyboard user never sees; the "?" sheet gets a glossary section for them.
OPTION B · BRIEF

Five destinations

Collapse to Today, Needs you (Finds and Unresolved as tabs), Mail (Inbox, Spoil, Contamination as tabs), Rules (Meshes, Strata, Never Hidden, Signals as tabs) and Director (chat, proposals, Expeditions). Activity and Settings move to the top bar. Fewer places to look; more tabs inside each.

Trade-off: tabs hide counts; Wroblewski's data says hidden sections lose traffic.
OPTION C · LEDGER

Keep the rail, add an account strip

No regrouping. A strip under the top bar shows the selected account: name, rung chip, "Right now" sentence, sync state, with a one-key step down. Counts follow the switcher.

Trade-off: leaves "Excavation" and the icons as they are.
What the evidence says. Wroblewski's "Obvious always wins": when Polar hid its sections, movement between them fell; keep every destination visible with counts. Spool: the gap between current knowledge (Gmail's words) and target knowledge (Riddle's) is where design happens, and the bridge must be unnoticed. Shulman et al. (2020): jargon hurts even with definitions, so glosses must say the Gmail effect, not define the metaphor. Superhuman recommends three to seven streams. Onyinye: teach the vocabulary once, first. The audit: the metaphor works for Find, Dig and Sift; it misleads for Strata and the icons contradict Spoil and Mesh. Kole Jain: the sidebar is "the spine of your product", each link an icon plus a short title so it can collapse and carry badges, settings and help at the bottom, and a tooltip on every icon because "the user won't understand all of our icons".
Recommendation: A. Under the Brief direction, B is the natural next step once A's glosses have done their work. Keep "Dig" as the noun; the gloss and the wizard carry it. Rename nothing in the engine.

Flow 3 · The daily loop

Summary, then Finds: the check-in that should take a minute

Today. The Summary is always "All Digs". Finds are grouped by type with a priority bar; the Find's plain-English reason is computed and never rendered, while the Why card leads with a monospace route walk 3. After resolving a Find opened with Enter, the cursor is invisible until the mouse is used 3. Lists cap at 200 with no paging, search, sort or multi-select 3. "Not important" is two different Actions 3. Resolve, Snooze, Retype and Not important with keys is good; the snooze picker is good.
OPTION A · BRIEF

The morning brief and a bounded review

The Summary becomes a brief for the selected account or all: "Since yesterday: 7 need you (3 respond, 2 pay, 2 decide) · 41 filed · 12 to Spoil, which Act would have archived · 5 Unresolved. Riddle noticed: 6 GitHub notifications keep landing in Unresolved." Every line is a link. A "Start review" button walks Finds then Unresolved one card at a time with the same keys, and ends with "You resolved 9 and taught 2 rules. Nothing else needs you." The rail badge counts decisions pending, not messages.

Trade-off: card mode suits small counts; past a few dozen the list (B) must remain one key away.
OPTION B · DECK

The triage deck

Finds as a reason-first list: the first line is the reason in the user's words ("Jamie asked for your review; a follow-up says it blocks the merge"), the second the subject and sender, then chips for type, rule and due. Tabs Needs you / Later / Done replace the Status filter. Single click selects and shows the preview; Enter opens; j/k always work; x selects; paging; sort within a group by due or priority; a palette that prints the keys. The Why card shows the reason sentence first and collapses the route walk.

Trade-off: reason lines make rows taller; the density budget must come from removing borders, not spacing.
OPTION C · LEDGER

Reason and rule on the row, nothing else moves

Keep the layout. Add the reason line and a "decided by Mesh v7" chip to each row, render the reason first in the detail, collapse the route walk, fix the dead end, make the Summary follow the switcher, rename the Why-card button.

Trade-off: leaves the 200 cap and the lack of bulk actions.
What the evidence says. Spool's trigger words: users succeed 72 percent of the time when their own words are on the page, 6 percent when not; a Find labelled only by type has no scent. GitHub prints a reason on every row; Copilot's why-headline was what Microsoft's users valued; Focused Inbox's silence is what they distrusted. Linear's Priority tab and Superhuman's Important/Other: two lists beat one ranking. Kulesza: explanations should be sound, complete and bounded (ten features did as well as fifty), which is the route walk collapsed under a sentence. Cowan: at most four unchunked decisions at once, which is the card review. Kahneman's peak-end: close the review with what was resolved. Wroblewski's one-thumb test: open, see, dispose of one item, leave in under a minute. Kole Jain on the row itself: let the data drive the form (types and rules as chips, counts and times right-aligned, subjects truncated, decided rows shaded), lay it out like a receipt on two hard edges without cards, and fix density with differentiation rather than white space, because "nobody reads a screen".
Recommendation: A and B together. They are the same data at two densities: the brief is the entry and the exit, the deck is the middle. The reason line uses the existing find.reason; the rule chip uses the classification's Mesh id and version already attached to corrections. The dead-end fix and the rename are a morning's work and should not wait for the rest.

Flow 4 · Unresolved

Deciding what Riddle could not

Today. A clear page: "Riddle could not decide. Deciding here is the fastest way to teach it." But f silently creates a Respond Find while the button opens a type menu 2; no multi-select or bulk decide, so 200 items after a week away is 200 decisions 2; nothing tells the user what the decision teaches or how many corrections lead to a proposal.
OPTION A · DECK

One at a time, four keys, and what it teaches

Unresolved as a card: sender, subject, the message summary, and the reason Riddle stopped ("no rule mentions this sender; two Signals disagreed"). Riddle's best guess is shown as a suggestion with its confidence, below the summary, not above it. Keys: 1 Find (opens the type picker), 2 Filed, 3 Spoil, 4 Contamination, h Never hide, s later. After each decision a line says what it taught: "Recorded. 3 similar from this sender; the Director will propose a rule at 5." An inline "also add a rule for this sender" toggle turns the decision into a sender-scoped rule.

Trade-off: showing the guess below the summary costs a glance; it is the point.
OPTION B · BRIEF

Clusters first

Group Unresolved by what stopped Riddle: the sender, the list, the header, the disagreement. A cluster header reads "12 from notifications@github.com: decide all as Filed?" and one action decides the cluster, records the corrections, and offers the Director's draft rule with a preview count. Singles fall through to the card flow.

Trade-off: a wrong cluster decision is twelve wrong decisions; Undo All must be on the toast.
OPTION C · MINIMAL

Fix the keys

f opens the type picker; x selects; a bulk decide toolbar; paging past 200. No cluster or teaching line.

Trade-off: the fastest fix; leaves the teaching invisible.
What the evidence says. Vaccaro et al. (2024): human plus AI teams win only where the human is the stronger judge, which is exactly the borderline case; Unresolved is the primary human task, not an error bucket. Buçinca et al. (2021): showing the AI's verdict after the person has looked cuts overreliance. Zhang, Liao and Bellamy (2020): a confidence number calibrates trust where an explanation does not. Kulesza: an undecided message is a too-few-examples case, so ask for a feature (the sender rule), not only a label. Linear's triage uses 1/2/3 verdicts. Femke van Schoonhoven: Unresolved is where clarity beats forced simplicity; show which Meshes fired and which Signal abstained. Spool: name the trigger word Riddle lacked.
Recommendation: A with B's cluster header. The reason Riddle stopped is already in the explanation trace (routes that returned Unknown, signals that disagreed); the teaching line needs the corrections count per Mesh, which the new mesh_corrections read exposes. The sender-rule toggle should create a small Mesh route through the normal preview-then-apply path, not a hidden override.
Effort MFrontend; cluster decide is a batched ActionFixes #15, #18

Flow 5 · Reading mail

Inbox, Spoil, Contamination and the message page

Today. Inbox, Spoil and Contamination rows open only on double-click or Enter; there is no preview pane; the message page has no next or previous, so triage is open, act, back, j 3. s snoozes 24 hours silently on messages but opens a picker on Finds 2. Review-page buttons act on the cursor row when nothing is checked 2. Gmail label ids show raw. The reply tree we just built is good; long threads have no collapse-all or jump-to-latest.
OPTION A · ANY DIRECTION

One list model everywhere

Single click selects and shows the preview pane, as Finds already does; Enter opens the full page; j/k move in either. The message page gets n/p and "3 of 41". s opens the picker everywhere. Toolbar buttons say what they act on: "Archive 1 (cursor)" or "Archive 3 selected". In Spoil and Contamination the preview shows the message summary first and Riddle's verdict and reason beneath it, with a "Not Spoil" that offers the sender scope. Gmail labels are named, not id'd. Threads get collapse-all and jump-to-latest, with the depth cap kept.

Trade-off: the preview pane costs width on a laptop; it collapses to a two-pane layout below 1100 px.
OPTION B · BRIEF

Sender digests in Spoil

Spoil grouped by sender: one row per sender with a count and the newest subject, expanding to the messages. "Not Spoil for this sender" is one action and one rule; "Never hide" sits beside it. Contamination stays flat but reason-first, with the evidence chips (auth failed, lookalike domain) on the row.

Trade-off: a sender with one message looks the same as one with forty; the count must carry the weight.
OPTION C · DECK

Full page with a queue

Keep the full-page message but add n/p, a queue position, and the verdict keys on the page. No preview pane.

Trade-off: still leaves the list to read anything.
What the evidence says. Jakob's law and the audit: two list behaviours in one app is the pattern break people remember (Onyinye). Apple Mail's sender digests and HEY's bundles collapse prolific senders to one row. Buçinca: in the views that can hide mail, show the content before the verdict. Slack's designers: one visible level with a count is what stays legible; Coding Horror argues against deep trees; keep the tree but give it collapse controls. Superhuman, Apple and Shortwave all scope corrections to the sender. Femke van Schoonhoven: a control must mean one clear thing, so the toolbar names its target. Kole Jain: secondary actions sit lower on "the spectrum of explicitness", revealed on hover and focus with a tooltip, and a disposition is optimistic, the way Gmail's delete "disappears instantly, assuming that the server request will succeed", with Undo as the safety net.
Recommendation: A, with B for Spoil. The preview pane reuses the message page's body renderer; sender grouping is a client-side group over the existing page. The snooze picker already exists on Finds. Label names need the Gmail label list, which the sync already fetches.

Flow 6 · Corrections

Correcting Riddle and seeing what it learned

Today. Corrections exist everywhere under different names (Not Spoil, Restore, Correct…, Decide, Not important twice) 3. The toast never says whether Gmail changed 3. A correction "never changes a rule by itself" but nothing shows corrections accumulating or which Mesh they count against. There is no scope choice: a correction is always one message. Undo semantics are documented well.
OPTION A · LEDGER

The scoped correction sheet

Every correction opens a small in-place sheet under the row, not a modal: "Move to Spoil: this message · this sender (14) · this domain (31)", each with the count it would apply to now and going forward. A line states the Gmail effect for this account ("archived in Gmail" or "Riddle only, Observe"). The toast reads "Moved to Spoil · rule added for this sender · Riddle only" with Undo. Sender and domain rules appear under Settings → Mail rules → Your corrections, editable and removable. A row a correction decided shows a "Your rule" chip.

Trade-off: one more choice per correction; the default (this message) is pre-selected and Enter takes it.
OPTION B · DECK

The editable Why card

Every clause in the route walk becomes actionable: "make this pass", "raise the threshold", "add sender to Never Hidden". The change re-simulates at once, shows "would change 37 messages", and Apply creates a Mesh version through the normal path. This is explanatory debugging in full; it belongs on the Mesh page too (Flow 9).

Trade-off: the deepest option; needs the simulator wired to the card.
OPTION C · MINIMAL

Rename and tell

One name per Action, the Gmail effect in every toast, and a corrections count on the Mesh page. No scope choice.

Trade-off: corrections stay one message at a time.
What the evidence says. The universal correction primitive in Gmail tabs, Apple, Spark, Shortwave, Superhuman and Proton is sender-scoped and persistent; the user's mental model is "that sender", not "that weight". Shortwave lists every resulting rule under Settings; Outlook's hidden override list is the documented source of distrust. Kulesza's principles: actionable, reversible, always honoured, incremental, and "what happens if I change this" as the top question, which the counts answer. Amershi G9 and G16: efficient correction, and convey the consequences. Dietvorst (2018): letting people adjust the algorithm even slightly restores use after an error. Wroblewski: "even just seeing where information comes from gives people a real trust boost." Kole Jain's container ladder: a simple, non-blocking context belongs in a popover, not a page, with the primary action at the top and the dangerous one lower, which is exactly the in-place sheet.
Recommendation: A now, B on the Mesh page (Flow 9). Sender and domain rules can be small generated Mesh routes in a per-account "Your corrections" Mesh, which keeps them visible, versioned and undoable through the existing machinery instead of a second rule system. The Gmail effect line already exists as gmailEffect in the account model.

Flow 7 · Trust and autonomy

Knowing what Riddle will do to your mailbox, and letting it do more

Today. The rung is shown only in Settings 3; a user with Work at Act and Personal at Observe sees the same chrome for both. Toasts are identical at every rung 3. The Shadow card's "Review proposals" opens a plain inbox with no agree or disagree while the toolbar acts for real 3. "Nothing is hidden" on degraded paths, the "Right now" sentence, and Trusted behind a hold are all good.
OPTION A · LEDGER

Rung in the chrome, effect in every toast

The account switcher shows "Personal · Observe · synced 2m ago". Clicking opens a popover with the "Right now" sentence, the ladder as current rung and one recommended next rung (not four peers), and a one-key step down. Every toast and Activity line states the Gmail effect. Activity shows queued writes as a distinct state from written ones. Confirmations for Gmail writes use verb plus noun with a count: "Archive 42 messages in Gmail?" with the button "Archive", and a line saying what Undo covers. Approve and Send lose their autofocus.

Trade-off: none worth naming; this is the floor.
OPTION B · BRIEF

The trust card and the seducible moment

A card on the Summary: "Last 7 days on Personal: 214 decisions, 3 you corrected, 0 undone. Riddle would have archived 41 to Spoil." Per-rule precision beneath it ("Promotions v4: reversed 1 of 60"). Only after a clean run does a one-line offer appear, dismissable: "Riddle got the last 40 Spoil calls right. Let it archive Spoil on Personal?" with the rung and the undo guarantee stated. A rule whose reversal rate climbs is demoted to Suggest and the user is told. Shadow review becomes a list with Agree and Disagree per row that feeds the same numbers.

Trade-off: the offer must never be a nag; once dismissed it waits for the next clean run.
OPTION C · LEDGER, DEEPER

Autonomy per rule

The rung lives on each Mesh route rather than the account: Promotions may archive while Newsletters only labels. Promotion and demotion happen per rule on evidence. Lee and See's "resolution": trust should track the capability of each function.

Trade-off: an engine change to the projection gate and the settings model; the biggest bet on this page.
What the evidence says. Spool's seducible moment: the right time for a secondary ask is right after the mission is satisfied; offer more autonomy then, never in Settings at startup. Lee and See: calibrated trust needs the automation's purpose, process and performance on display; per-rule precision is performance. Parasuraman and Riley: false alarms produce disuse, so a rule whose reversals climb must step down. Amershi G2, G17, G18: how well it works, global controls, notify about changes. Zander Whitehurst: "'Are you sure' tells people nothing"; verb plus noun. Nielsen: focus the safe action. Onyinye: in a low-trust market people want to know where their money is; show "would have archived 41" before any write. Android: the app proposes importance, only the user raises it. Kole Jain: keep the primary colour on the non-destructive action so people can "trust that it's always that way", destructive stays red, and grade the rung copy on the four-level rubric so it says how it helps ("Riddle files newsletters so you never see them") rather than what it does.
Recommendation: A and B; C when Meshes multiply. Per-rule precision comes from Activity: reversals per Mesh version over a window. The offer is a Summary card driven by that number, and accepting it runs the existing autonomy Action. The Shadow review list reuses the Unresolved card with Agree and Disagree keys.

Flow 8 · The Director

Talking to the Director, and what a proposal looks like

Today. The Director opens on an empty chat with six suggestions that send at once. Enter sends; there is no Stop 2. "Talk to the Director about this" auto-sends a prompt containing raw ids 2. Proposals and their badge live in session memory; Dismiss never reaches the backend 3. Approve is autofocused 3. No simulation is shown before Apply, although the Mesh editor has one. Tool-call cards, per-turn cost and the untrusted-content banner are good. Answers cannot be selected 3.
OPTION A · BRIEF

Starts with an answer, lives where the evidence is

The Director page opens with a generated note ("Since Tuesday I noticed: 6 GitHub CI failures landed in Unresolved; deals@retailer keeps being corrected to Spoil") and two or three proposal cards. The same cards appear inline on an Unresolved cluster header and on a Spoil sender row. Chat is a collapsed pane beneath. ⌘↵ sends, Enter is a newline; a Stop button; "Talk to the Director about this" prefills the composer without sending and names the message by subject and sender, never by id.

Trade-off: the opening note costs a model call per visit; cache it per day.
OPTION B · DECK

The proposal queue

Proposals are a persisted queue with an unread count in the rail, fetched on launch. Keys: 1 apply, 2 adjust (opens the Mesh editor prefilled), 3 decline with a reason (an Action, so the backend forgets it too). The approval step focuses Reject; Apply needs a deliberate press.

Trade-off: none; this is the persistence fix with keys on top.
OPTION C · LEDGER

The proposal as a case

Every card reads: the problem with counts ("6 CI failures in Unresolved this week"); the evidence (the messages, by subject, with hover previews); the proposed change in one verb-plus-noun line ("Route GitHub ci_activity with a failed conclusion to Find: Do; the rest to Filed"); options considered and rejected; the simulation ("would change 14 messages: 6 to Find, 8 to Filed; 0 currently in Finds; no Never Hidden sender touched"); the DSL diff under a disclosure; how success will be judged ("Unresolved from GitHub should fall to 0").

Trade-off: longer cards; the disclosure keeps the diff out of the way.
What the evidence says. Wroblewski: "people regularly struggle with knowing what to ask", suggested questions did the heavy lifting, citations give a trust boost, and the agent's work should be collapsed with each step linked to its result; a Director rail item competing with Finds is a bolted-on feature. Femke van Schoonhoven: proposals as "options compared"; approval must surface judgment, not lint, or "people will route around it". Sharon Onyinye: case-study shape. Zander Whitehurst: verb plus noun and "Apply rule", not "Approve". Bansal et al.: explanations raise acceptance whether right or wrong, so the card leads with counts and the simulation rather than persuasion. Dietvorst: an "Adjust" affordance restores use; the IKEA effect says people value rules they finished themselves. Notion Mail's sample-and-tick and Front's example approval preview matches before a rule goes live. Kole Jain: chain the tiny decisions Notion-style with a skip, and guard model-generated UI the way vibe-coded UI needs guarding, since AI "has a hard time" with the invisible layer of states and tooltips: the Director emits DSL and copy, never colour, emoji or layout, and each count appears once.
Recommendation: all three, in this order: B (persistence and keys), C (the card), then A (the opening note and inline placement). The simulation already exists (simulate_mesh); the card calls it on arrival. The Director's prompt already tells it to simulate before proposing; the card makes that visible.

Flow 9 · Meshes

Editing a rule by hand

Today. A text editor with live parse, line markers, a change note, version diffs, Preview impact with cost, and a hold before a Spoil-proposing Mesh can act. The line-number gutter drifts once the Mesh scrolls 2. The DSL is learnt from a twenty-line syntax box; no completion, no hover on a Signal, no match counts 2. Nothing shows precedence between Meshes; with ten, the user cannot see which wins 2. The mock parser in the browser does not understand WHEN blocks.
OPTION A · DECK

The live-matching editor

Validate on blur, not per keystroke. Beside each route, "matches 118 now" with the first three subjects on hover; a route matching zero or thousands says so before Apply. Hover a Signal name for its definition and calibration; ⌘K in the editor inserts facts, headers and Signals from a list. The gutter scrolls with the text.

Trade-off: per-route counts need a simulation per parse; debounce to blur and cache by text hash.
OPTION B · BRIEF

Plain language in, DSL out

A "Describe the rule" box above the editor: "file GitHub CI passes, find the failures". The Director drafts the DSL beside it with the simulation; the user edits and applies. The text stays the source of truth; the description is a starting point.

Trade-off: a model call per draft; the Director already has the tools.
OPTION C · LEDGER

Structure across Meshes

A Structure view over all Meshes: evaluation order, which Mesh decides a sample message, decisions and corrections per Mesh, per-rule precision, a link from every Why card to the version diff that decided.

Trade-off: most useful past five Meshes; today there are three.
What the evidence says. Wroblewski's inline-validation study: validate after the field, only where errors are likely, keep success marks; and "people don't know how to guide it", so plain language in with the rewrite visible teaches the grammar by refinement. Zander Whitehurst: learn by doing, inside your own files; live matching in Observe is the sandbox. Kulesza: the top information need was "what will happen if I change this"; per-route counts answer it before commit. Notion and Front previewed matches before a rule went live; Shortwave lists every rule. The IKEA effect: let the Director start the rule and the user finish it.
Recommendation: A and B; C when there are more than five Meshes. Match counts come from the existing detailed simulation, keyed by route. The description box calls the Director with the existing update-mesh tooling but returns text into the editor instead of a proposal. Fix the gutter first; it is a CSS change.

Flow 10 · Settings

The four tabs, and the seams between them

Today. The tabs are the clearest surface in the app. Gaps: no reconnect (Flow 1); no ⌘,; "Everything here is saved through the Action layer" and "The Action layer gave no count" are system-centric; the Mail rules tab says "You edit this list; Riddle cannot" although the Director can add to it after approval 2; the notification threshold is an unexplained number 2; free-text model ids are validated only by Probe; "Review and relaunch" has no relaunch 2.
OPTION A · ANY DIRECTION

Fix the seams

⌘, opens Settings. Every "change this in Settings" phrase in the app is a link to the field. A copy pass removes system nouns: "Saved and listed in Activity", "Riddle could not count these messages; try again". The threshold is explained with examples from the user's own mail ("P82 was Jamie's review request; P40 was a newsletter") and a preview: "last week this would have notified you 4 times". Model ids get a picker fed by a probe. The Never Hidden copy says what the Director may do. "Your corrections" lists sender and domain rules (Flow 6).

Trade-off: none; these are seams.
OPTION B · DECK

Search and ownership

A search field that hides non-matching settings; sections regrouped by ownership: this app, this account, the rules. Useful once settings outgrow four tabs.

Trade-off: premature at the current size.
What the evidence says. The house playbook: settings cross-links as navigation objects; ⌘,; toggles, not checkboxes. Femke van Schoonhoven: a control must mean one clear thing. Spool: current knowledge is the user's, not the team's; "Action layer" is the team's word. Amershi G18: notify about changes, which the Update Preview already does well.
Recommendation: A. The threshold preview is a count over last week's Finds by priority, which the store has.

Flow 11 · Search and the palette

Ask Riddle, search, and finding any command

Today. "Ask Riddle" guesses from a regex: is:unread, show me everything from adam and find the receipt all go to the Director and are sent at once, at real cost, with no chance to edit 3. The hint is 11 px text. g-chords and t/u are discoverable only from the "?" sheet; the rail shows no key hints 2. The one shortcut table driving keys and help is a strength to keep.
OPTION A · ANY DIRECTION

Search by default, ask by intent, never auto-send

Anything with an operator or plain words searches. A trailing "?" or a leading "ask" routes to the Director but only prefills and focuses the composer. The search results page offers "Ask the Director about these 14 results" as a button. The hint becomes a visible segmented toggle: Search · Ask.

Trade-off: one more keypress for a question; it is the keypress that costs money.
OPTION B · DECK

The palette that prints the keys

⌘K opens a palette over the same shortcut table: navigation ("Go to Finds · g f"), actions valid for the current row ("Resolve · e"), search, and "Ask the Director…" as a command. Each entry shows its key, so people graduate to the key. Invalid actions are hidden, not greyed.

Trade-off: one more surface to keep in step; it must be generated from the table, never hand-written.
What the evidence says. Superhuman's and Raycast's palettes print the shortcut beside every entry and users graduate to the key; Superhuman boosts commands valid in the current state and hides the rest. Maggie Appleton: a command bar is only useful if it finds things by the words people actually use. Spool: search is where people go when links have failed them; a misrouted search is a scent failure at real cost. Wroblewski: suggested questions do the heavy lifting, so the results page can suggest the question. Kole Jain, designing a macOS utility: a floating search that appears where you need it "and then immediately get out of the way", and a shortcut cheat sheet in Help because shortcuts are "very powerful but also easy to forget".
Recommendation: A and B. The palette is a view over shortcuts.ts and the per-page action tables that already exist; the routing fix is a few lines in the search box.

Flow 12 · Notifications and rhythm

When Riddle should interrupt, and when it should wait

Today. One global priority threshold; a notification for every Find at or above it; a Test button that works. Nothing batches; nothing distinguishes an allow-listed sender from a threshold; the badge counts Finds.
OPTION A · BRIEF

Allow-list alerts, a brief for the rest

Immediate notifications only for Never Hidden senders and for Finds of type Pay or Respond with a due date inside a day. Everything else waits for a brief at two or three user-chosen times (default 9:00 and 16:00): "7 need you, 5 Unresolved, 41 filed." The dock badge counts decisions pending, never messages. Notifications say the reason, not the subject, so a locked screen shows nothing private.

Trade-off: a Find raised at 10:00 waits until 16:00 unless it is allow-listed or due; that is the design.
OPTION B · MINIMAL

The threshold, explained

Keep the single threshold, explain it with examples from the user's mail, and preview "last week this would have notified you 4 times".

Trade-off: still a stream, not batches.
What the evidence says. Fitz et al. (2019), a randomised trial: three notification batches a day improved attention, mood and control; hourly equalled no change; none at all raised anxiety. Kushlev and Dunn: three checks a day lowered stress with the same mail handled. Mark et al. (2016): notification-driven checkers fared worse. Pielot: people re-check when they fear missing something, so keep an allow-list bypass. HEY: off by default, no badge; Gmail notifies Primary only; Linear digests only what is still unread; Slack VIPs bypass quiet hours. Peak-end: the brief closes with what was handled.
Recommendation: A with B's preview on the settings page. The brief reuses the Summary's copy; the schedule is two settings fields; allow-listing is the existing Never Hidden list plus the due-date rule.

The sequence

How we will know

Outcome metrics, not satisfaction scores

Spool's argument against NPS and Onyinye's success-first process point the same way: measure the outcome the product promises. All of these can be computed locally from Activity and shown in a private stats panel; none needs a survey.

Time from launch to an empty Finds listThe one-minute check-in test, measured
Corrections per hundred classificationsFalling means the rules are learning
Undo rate per dispositionA frustration proxy in Spool's sense; rising means surprise
Unresolved share of incoming mailFalling means Riddle decides more; watch it does not fall by hiding
Proposals applied, adjusted, declinedAdjusted is the healthiest signal: people finishing the Director's rules
Keyboard-only completion of the daily loopAny mouse use in the review is a dead end to find
Never Hidden violationsMust be zero, always
Days at each rung per accountPromotion on evidence, demotion on reversals
