Getting to your labels

Today the only way into a Gmail label is the Inbox's Label filter: a flat menu of 32 labels, children not under their parents, half of them empty. Four ways to give labels a place, each a working mockup on your own labels and counts. Click around; the list follows.

Your mailbox, as Riddle holds it
32 Gmail labels, 21 of them empty in Riddle's window; one branch (Work › Northwind › Github) holds 2,463 messages. 19 Strata, three of which share a name with a label (Newsletters, Receipts, Travel).
Judged against
Luke Wroblewski's Obvious Always Wins, Jared Spool's scent of information, Kole Jain's sidebar as the spine and progressive disclosure, Jesse Showalter's rail that disappears, and Hick's law as Zander Whitehurst and Showalter teach it.

OPTION A

A mail column, Mimestream-style

Choosing a mail view opens a second column beside the rail: Gmail's own places, the category tabs, every label as a tree with unread counts, then Riddle's Strata. Everything is visible, all the time.

For
  • Obvious: every place is on screen (Wroblewski)
  • Familiar from Gmail, Apple Mail, Mimestream (Jakob's law)
  • Counts on every row give scent (Spool)
Against
  • A second spine beside the rail costs 220px, every visit
  • 32 labels plus 19 Strata is a long list to scan (Hick)
  • Labels and Strata with the same name sit apart and compete
OPTION B

The rail opens in place

No new column: Inbox in the rail grows a disclosure triangle, and your labels unfold beneath it as a tree. One spine, as Kole Jain puts it; the mail list keeps its width.

For
  • One navigation spine, one active state
  • Costs no width; labels appear only when Inbox is open
  • Still visible, not behind a menu
Against
  • The rail grows long and pushes Rules and Director below the fold
  • A 150px rail truncates deep names (Work › Northwind › Github)
  • Mixes "where in the app" with "which mail", two kinds of place
OPTION C

A scope bar with a label picker

The list's header becomes a path: Inbox ▾ › Accounting ▾. Each step opens a picker you can type into. The labels you use most sit as chips under it. Built for the keyboard: g l opens the picker.

For
  • Scales to hundreds of labels; type to find one
  • The list keeps its full width
  • Fast for anyone who knows the name
Against
  • Hidden until opened: what you cannot see you do not use (Wroblewski)
  • Weak scent for labels you forgot you had (Spool)
  • Counts are only visible inside the picker
OPTION D

Places: one column that knows what matters

A's visible column, with Kole Jain's progressive disclosure. Pinned favourites first; then Topics, where a Gmail label and a Stratum with the same name are one row (marked with where they come from); then the rest of your labels, folded, with empty ones out of the way. The column collapses to icons (⌘ \), and C's picker is there for the keyboard (g l).

For
  • Visible like A, but short: about a dozen rows before you open anything (Hick)
  • Riddle's Strata and your labels finally meet; one "Receipts", not two
  • Every row is a trigger word with a count (Spool)
  • Collapses away when you want width (Showalter's disappearing rail)
Against
  • More to build than A: merging, pinning, a collapsed state
  • "Topics" is a new idea to learn (one tooltip covers it)

Recommendation: D, built in two steps.
  1. First, A done well. The visible column with the tree nested, unread counts, empty labels folded under "Show 21 empty", and the keyboard picker. This alone fixes today's problem.
  2. Then D's two ideas. Pinning (favourites to the top), and Topics, which merge a label and a Stratum of the same name, once that merge has been tried on real mail; then the collapse to icons.
Rejected: B, because a rail that unfolds mail places pushes the rest of the app away; C on its own, because hidden labels are forgotten labels. C's picker survives inside A and D.
